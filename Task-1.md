Natural and structured languages differ significantly in their learnability due to their underlying characteristics. Natural language is highly diverse, context-dependent, and semantically rich, where the meaning of a word often depends on long-range context. This makes it harder for models like MLPs to capture deeper relationships, leading to higher loss and overfitting. In contrast, structured languages such as C++ follow fixed grammatical and syntactic rules, making token sequences more predictable and easier to learn. As a result, models trained on structured data converge faster, generalize better, and achieve lower validation loss. In essence, natural language requires semantic understanding, while structured language primarily demands recognition of rule-based patterns.
